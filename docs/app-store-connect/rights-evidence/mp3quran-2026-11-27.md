# MP3Quran recording permissions

Verified November 27, 2026 for Quran Carplayer’s nine Hafs an Asim collections. The iPhone app streams individual recordings directly from the provider. A listener can also save individual recordings for later playback inside the app.

## Provider statements

The English contact page permits developers to copy material or use site URLs and directs developers to give Apple the provider's privacy page as evidence. [Contact notice](https://www.mp3quran.net/eng/contact-us) · [Page designated for Apple](https://mp3quran.net/eng/privacy)

The English privacy page's Copyrights section grants permission to “copy any material or use any link on the website.” [Copyright notice](https://www.mp3quran.net/privacy-en.html)

## Collections in this build

The catalog identifies Maher Al-Muaiqly (moshaf 102), Mishary Rashid Alafasy (123), Abdul Rahman Al-Sudais (54), Saud Al-Shuraim (31), Saad Al-Ghamdi (30), Mahmoud Khalil Al-Hussary (118), Mohammed Siddiq Al-Minshawi (112), Abdulbasit Abdulsamad (53), and Yasser Al-Dosari (92). Each includes chapters 1 through 114. The snapshot records the exact server and reciter identifier separately for each collection. ## Submission decision

Quran Carplayer is free to listeners and has no ads or purchases. The launch target includes Canada, the U.S. and as many other eligible storefronts as possible. The app streams the provider’s original files and lets a listener keep an unmodified copy privately inside the app. The public copying notice gives no territorial limit. It leaves open the scope of the underlying recording permissions, persistent offline storage, and the audio CDN’s handling of IP addresses and recording requests. Confirm those points for the intended storefronts before finalizing the content rights and privacy declarations.

The current build includes seven portraits with credit and license records. Minshawi and Mishary use geometric chapter artwork generated on the phone. [Artwork source and license records](../../ARTWORK_ATTRIBUTION.md)

## Alternatives considered before provider outreach

- **An explicitly reusable recording.** The Commons contributor Ibrahimmusa4's Al-Fatihah file is identified as the creator's own recording and released under CC0. It supplies a clear route to bundling or hosting that particular performance. It covers one chapter and a different voice. [Original recording and dedication](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:AlF%C4%81tihatulKit%C4%81b.ogg)
- **A service with explicit offline terms.** Quran Foundation allows streaming from its audio hosts and user-requested downloads held privately on the device. The app must credit the Foundation and reciter, implement deletion, and for recordings in Content Sync, synchronize at least weekly when reachable and apply removals and URL changes. The terms prohibit rehosting. Switching this app would require catalog, attribution, synchronization and privacy changes. The [chapter catalog comparison](quran-foundation-catalog.md) records coverage for the nine requested reciters. [Developer FAQ and terms](https://api-docs.quran.foundation/docs/tutorials/faq/#can-my-app-let-users-download-recitations-for-offline-listening)
- **A service with published log limits.** Al Quran Cloud permits streaming, embedding and downloading for personal and educational use and states a 29-day maximum log retention. Its recordings remain subject to the reciters' rights. Its policy cannot answer retention questions for MP3Quran. [Provider terms](https://alquran.cloud/terms-and-conditions)

A free, nonprofit release can satisfy a noncommercial restriction where one applies. Permission to distribute the underlying sound recordings still depends on the applicable grant. The current build keeps the nine previously selected collections.
