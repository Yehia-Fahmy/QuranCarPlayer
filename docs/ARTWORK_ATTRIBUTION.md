# Chapter artwork attribution

The app generates chapter artwork on-device from eight bundled portrait sources. Each portrait is cropped or reframed, resized, and color-treated, then combined with the canonical surah number and bilingual chapter names. Generated artwork remains available offline. Reframed images use a blurred extension of the same source photo to preserve comfortable spacing in square artwork.

For Mohammed Siddiq Al-Minshawi, the app can also download a 1954 portrait when StoreKit confirms a Canadian or U.S. storefront. The default geometric artwork appears in all other storefronts and while the photo is unavailable. The public [Now Playing](artwork/now-playing.jpg) and [CarPlay list](artwork/carplay-list.jpg) previews show that worldwide default.

The eight sources include three CC BY-SA 4.0 photos, one CC BY-NC 2.0 photo, three Government Open Data License – India grants and one worldwide public-domain dedication. The license terms for both CC BY-SA 4.0 and GODL-India grant worldwide copyright permission subject to their attribution and other conditions. ## Mishary Rashid Alafasy

The Islamic Cultural Center Graz photographed its May 3, 2017 visit from Sheikh Mishary Rashid Alafasy. In the selected group photograph he faces the camera in the center, dressed in a brown bisht and a red-and-white keffiyeh. ## Mohammed Siddiq Al-Minshawi — Canada and U.S. storefronts

Egypt’s *Radio Magazine*, issue 11, published this black-and-white portrait on June 12, 1954. The photographer is unidentified. Wikimedia Commons documents the magazine issue, the source scan and the Egyptian public-domain rationale. Its U.S. table covers Egyptian non-creative photographs first published before 1981, whose source-country protection had expired by the U.S. restoration date. This is the historical basis used for the U.S. storefront. [Publication and copyright record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Al-Minshawi_1954.jpg) · [U.S. restoration rule](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html#104a)

Canada’s former Copyright Act section 6.1 gave an unknown-author work a maximum term of 50 years after the end of its first publication year, subject to its exception for an author identified during the term. On the documented anonymous 1954 publication, that term ended by December 31, 2004. The later extension expressly preserved earlier expiries. [Former section 6.1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/section-6.1-20021231.html) · [Transition and non-revival, section 34](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-10.65/section-34.html)

The photo is outside the app bundle. When Minshawi’s artwork is requested, StoreKit’s country code gates a download from Wikimedia. The app verifies the file against the reviewed scan and can reuse a local cached copy while StoreKit confirms Canada or the U.S. An unknown storefront hides the photo. A confirmed change to a different storefront clears the cached file. Both cases use the original geometric design. The chapter number, bilingual titles and credit are rendered on-device. This storefront rule applies only to the image. All nine reciters’ audio remains available to the app. [Wikimedia scan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/da/Al-Minshawi_1954.jpg)

Worldwide photographic reuse remains unresolved. The 2022 Commons upload describes a different archival photo as the uploader’s own work without identifying the original photographer. Indonesia’s national archives identify Minshawi in a November 15, 1955 photograph and publish a reproduction-permission process. A worldwide grant from the rights holder of a specific image could extend the portrait to other storefronts. [2022 upload](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Qari_Minshawi.png) · [Indonesian archive record](https://www.anri.go.id/en/publications/virtual-exhibition?page=11) · [Archive reproduction service](https://www.anri.go.id/layanan-publik/arsip-statis) · [Al-Minshawi family site](https://www.alminshawi.com/en)
